# DRUM + Meter: competition, pricing and monthly options

Research: 9 November 2026. Build: DRUM 0.26.1. Recommendations only; no prices or billing settings deployed.

## Recommendation and commercial decision

Research checked 9 November 2026 against DRUM 0.26.1 source/build and the current local Meter release documentation. This is a commercial recommendation, not a configured offer, financial forecast or statement that new licensing features already exist.

| Offer | Recommended customer price | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DRUM perpetual | GBP 79 regular; GBP 59 launch | Focused sample-to-mix instrument, major-version ownership |
| DRUM + Meter Pro perpetual | GBP 99 regular; GBP 79 launch | Create the drum mix, then measure the complete track |
| Bundle rent-to-own pilot | 12 monthly payments of GBP 8.25 = GBP 99 | A finite route to ownership, not perpetual rental |
| Recurring membership, later | Test GBP 4.99/month or GBP 49/year | Only with continuing content/service value; not the primary launch offer |

The strongest initial model is a perpetual product with a useful Meter Pro bundle. Offer instalments before trying to make two relatively affordable plug-ins support an indefinite subscription. A membership becomes credible when it delivers something valuable each month, not simply continued permission to open yesterday's work.

Proposed DJL prices are GBP consumer-facing prices, modelled as including VAT where applicable. Competitor prices remain in their displayed currencies. No exchange-rate conversion or tax equivalence is assumed. Current promotions, geography and checkout tax can materially change comparison.

Do not publish commercial promises beyond the verified release: the latest tested DRUM package is Mac VST3, with universal compilation but Apple Silicon runtime testing. Factory-content scale, other formats, completed Windows verification and paid subscription entitlements must not be implied by the report.

## What DRUM can now sell

Position DRUM as a visual sample-to-mix workbench for producers who already own samples: organise them, shape the main sound and each effect feed, balance the kit and reuse the recipe. The most persuasive demonstration is audible separation: a dry kick or snare stays unchanged while its reverb feed becomes shorter, darker or differently pitched.

| Implemented value | Customer outcome | Evidence/limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Instrument/group/return mixer | Balance sounds locally, then groups and shared spaces | Real mixing controls and meters; final whole-track fader remains in the DAW |
| Six independent send shapes per instrument | Different envelope, filter and pitch before each shared return | Audio-isolation checks; edited feeds bypass the main instrument Chain |
| Reusable settings without audio | Apply a known processing recipe to a different sample | 0.26.1 settings files preserve target identity/audio; full exports remain separate |
| Free envelopes and useful waveform editing | Make detailed shapes without committing to an ADSR template | Four neutral reset points; point/curve editing; Fit and Shift-wheel display zoom |
| Built-in + hosted VST3 chains | Keep useful processing with the instrument | 17 built-ins; third-party dependencies still required |
| Library/kit and Live workflow | Build reusable collections and work across tracks | One played kit/track, companion integration and independent Float windows |
| Meter companion proposition | Move from individual drum balance to whole-mix evidence | Meter remains a separate product; no automatic DRUM internal-bus telemetry is being claimed |

This is meaningful value beyond the old GBP 49 entry-level proposal. It does not mean every recent feature adds a separate price premium. Float, save reliability, no clicks, clear readouts and correct scrolling are expected product quality. The premium rests on a clear creative workflow and reusable sound/space design.

Proposed message: "Keep the punch. Shape the space. Mix the kit." Follow it with a 60-second before/after demonstration, not a claim that no competitor can make the same sound.

DRUM is strongest when the buyer wants detailed own-sample editing, explicit pre-return shaping, grouped mixing and reusable processing. It is weaker if the buyer mainly wants automatic sonic sample maps, hundreds of ready-made grooves or a deep velocity-layered library.

Sources: [TAL-Drum](https://tal-software.com/products/tal-drum), [Wave Alchemy Triaz](https://www.wavealchemy.co.uk/product/triaz/), [Toontrack EZdrummer 3](https://www.toontrack.com/product/ezdrummer-3/), [Ableton Racks manual](https://www.ableton.com/en/manual/instrument-drum-and-effect-racks/).

Independent send shaping is a strong interaction design and convenience story. The reviewed competitor marketing pages do not establish that it is unique. Avoid "the only sampler that..." until equivalent workflows and manuals have been tested directly.

For Live users, the question is not whether six returns are new - Drum Rack already has those. The case for DRUM is whether selecting a feed, drawing its shape and preserving its recipe is clearer and faster for that buyer than building and maintaining parallel racks. That needs user testing, not just a feature checklist.

## Feature comparison and positioning discipline

| Buyer job | DRUM current position | Competitive pressure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Play layered acoustic realism | One sample per instrument; not a deep multilayer acoustic engine | EZdrummer/Battery/layered samplers better address this job |
| Discover forgotten sounds automatically | Library/group organisation, not a verified AI similarity map | Atlas and XO make discovery a headline strength |
| Design separate effect excitation per sound | Independent A-F envelope/filter/pitch context with direct waveform selection | Parallel routing can reproduce parts of the outcome; streamlined workflow is the claim |
| Mix a large own-sample kit | Instrument/group/return controls, explicit output routing | Speedrum and DAW mixers are strong alternatives |
| Reuse processing on different audio | Settings-only presets preserve the target sample/identity | Common preset concepts already exist; portability and clarity are execution advantages |
| Monitor the finished track | Meter Pro is a separate complementary tool | Free/low-cost metering means the bundle needs a specific workflow, not basic meters alone |

A pad count comparison is especially misleading: DRUM's 88 single-sample instruments are not equivalent to 64 pads with 16 layers each. Nor is a processed waveform preview equivalent to every hosted effect being rendered into that picture. Commercial demonstrations must state where the signal is tapped.

The bundle should remain modular. A producer may want Meter without a drum sampler, or DRUM without a metering purchase. Preserve standalone licences and existing-owner paths rather than forcing unrelated purchases.

This report uses the dated note as the planning baseline, not a claim to have inspected a live DJL checkout. Resolve the catalogue before publishing comparisons.

## Price justification and a clean offer ladder

| Price point | Assessment |
| --- | --- |
| GBP 39-49 | Plausible founder/limited preview pricing, but makes a complex sound/mix tool look like a basic pad sampler and leaves less room for support/content work. |
| GBP 59 launch | Accessible trial-to-purchase decision while the brand builds trust. A credible introductory price without deep artificial discounting. |
| GBP 79 regular | Recommended working retail once commercial onboarding, licensing, regression/host testing and documentation are dependable. |
| GBP 99+ DRUM alone | Harder to defend now against mature products, bundled content, layering and broad format support. Revisit only with evidence of willingness to pay and a stronger release. |

The GBP 79 recommendation is a judgment about a finished release with this workflow, not a valuation derived mechanically from development hours. A current Mac-only development build has a smaller addressable market and more uncertainty than a proven multi-platform commercial product. Passing local tests is necessary quality evidence, not a certification.

At the official UK XO reference of GBP 127, DRUM at GBP 79 is GBP 48 lower, but XO includes substantial content/discovery value. Against EUR 99 Speedrum or USD 99 Atlas, currency/tax differences prevent a precise percentage claim. Those products still show why GBP 79 is a competitive mid-market ambition rather than an unchallenged bargain.

Perpetual proposal: ownership of the purchased major version, maintenance/bug fixes for that line, clear paid-major-upgrade terms and an offline-friendly activation policy. Do not promise free lifetime major updates if there is no funded plan to deliver them.

1. Use a usable trial, not a crippled interface that hides the main value.
2. Demonstrate unchanged dry audio while a send is shaped, then transfer the recipe to another sample.
3. Show mixer hierarchy and float behaviour in an actual Live session.
4. Test GBP 59 and GBP 79 with comparable qualified audiences before treating either as proven willingness to pay.

| Comparison | Amount | Interpretation |

| Separate with current recorded Pro offer | GBP 79 + GBP 39.99 = GBP 118.99 | Bundle GBP 99 saves GBP 19.99 (16.8%) |
| Launch separate basis | GBP 59 + GBP 39.99 = GBP 98.99 | Launch bundle GBP 79 saves GBP 19.99 (20.2%) |
| Existing Meter Pro owner proposal | DRUM GBP 49 regular / GBP 39 launch | Avoid making an existing customer repurchase Meter; final credit rules should prevent obviously worse offers |

Bundle the desktop Pro entitlement, not an ambiguously named "full suite". Android purchases and the Tap extension have separate existing commercial rules; include them only if the offer explicitly grants those entitlements. No assumption is made here that a DRUM purchase currently unlocks any Meter product.

GBP 99 is a compact, understandable whole-workflow offer. A GBP 79 launch gives a reason to try both without inflating DRUM's standalone price. Keep the saving calculation tied to the actual offer the customer could buy, not a superseded list price.

Owner discounts/credits are proposals. Where promotions overlap, show the cheapest eligible route and do not stack credits that make a bundle nonsensical. Measure how many buyers actually use both products before investing in a deeper integrated product tier.

## Subscriptions: where there is and is not room

There is room for recurring revenue, but two stable low-cost utilities do not automatically justify indefinite rental. A producer comparing a GBP 99 perpetual bundle with GBP 4.99/month reaches that gross purchase amount after about 20 months. A recurring offer must then remain useful enough to renew voluntarily.

| Model | Proposed role | Recommendation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Software rental only | GBP 4.99/month or GBP 49/year for DRUM + Meter Pro | Pilot only; weak retention case without meaningful recurring additions |
| Rent-to-own | GBP 8.25 x 12 = GBP 99 bundle, then perpetual ownership | Preferred monthly option to test first; a finite commitment and clear end state |
| Content/support membership for owners | Potential GBP 3.99/month or GBP 39/year; paid core licences stay owned | Stronger long-term model if sound/preset releases are consistent and valuable |
| Large all-tools subscription | GBP 9.99+ for only the present pair | Not recommended: stronger catalogue competitors already exist at similar headline amounts in their own currencies |

Kilohearts advertises USD 9.99/month for its full plug-in/content catalogue and a USD 100 subscriber reward after 12 months under its stated terms. Splice describes finite rent-to-own with ownership after completing payments. XO's UK page also offers an ownership payment schedule. These demonstrate that monthly payment and indefinite subscription are different products.

Sources: [Kilohearts Subscription](https://kilohearts.com/products/kilohearts_subscription), [Splice Rent-to-Own explanation](https://support.splice.com/en/articles/8652687-about-rent-to-own), [XLN Audio XO](https://www.xlnaudio.com/products/xo).

Start with the buyer's concern: "Will my projects still work?" A finite payment path or an owned core plus optional content is easier to explain than charging forever for two plug-ins whose main processing already runs locally.

Do not rename a software rental as rent-to-own unless payments really accrue to a defined perpetual entitlement. Do not call the Kilohearts reward coupon an automatic permanent licence for its entire catalogue.

## What a credible membership would deliver

1. Publish a realistic release cadence: for example one well-produced kit family plus processing/send-shaping recipes per month, with short how/why demonstrations. This is a proposal, not an existing content commitment.
2. Use instrument settings presets as part of the recurring value: a Dry punch / Dark room / Wide echo recipe pack can work on sounds the customer already owns.
3. Offer member education or live sessions only at a cadence the team can reliably support.
4. Keep downloaded content usage rights and project-recall terms explicit; use only content you own or have licensed for redistribution.

For owners, cancellation should stop future membership benefits while their owned plug-ins keep working. A software-rental pilot needs a separately engineered project-recall policy, such as allowing old project states to render without new editing after cancellation. Recall-only licensing, subscription expiry/grace, recurring billing and owner-credit logic are not implemented by the current DRUM build.

A settings preset system lowers the cost of useful recurring releases: the product can teach a sound-design method instead of relying solely on a growing sample catalogue. That is a plausible advantage, but the samples/presets still need curation, musical value and reliable licensing.

Do not quietly make bug fixes a paid-only benefit or remotely disable a customer's already-owned perpetual licence. Make the membership optional. A failed renewal should not unexpectedly replace or erase a saved sound. Test offline use and predictable cancellation before taking recurring payments.

## Unit economics: illustrative, not a forecast

Model assumptions: UK-style VAT-inclusive customer prices with 20% VAT; payment/platform charges assumed at 6% of gross plus GBP 0.30 per transaction; refund reserve 3% of ex-VAT revenue; variable support/fulfilment allowance GBP 3 per DRUM sale or GBP 4.50 per bundle. These costs are assumptions, not a quote from a merchant provider. GOV.UK verifies the standard 20% rate; actual tax mix and business treatment need the real sales setup.

Sources: [GOV.UK VAT rates](https://www.gov.uk/vat-rates).

| Offer | Customer pays | Ex-VAT revenue | Contribution before CAC/overhead |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DRUM launch | GBP 59.00 | GBP 49.17 | GBP 40.85 |
| DRUM regular | GBP 79.00 | GBP 65.83 | GBP 55.82 |
| Bundle regular | GBP 99.00 | GBP 82.50 | GBP 69.29 |

Formula: contribution = gross / 1.20 - (0.06 x gross + 0.30) - 0.03 x gross / 1.20 - variable support. It excludes development, fixed content creation, salaries, income/corporation tax and advertising/customer-acquisition cost (CAC). It is not profit.

For GBP 4.99/month rental, assuming GBP 1.50 variable support/content/serving cost per active subscriber, contribution is about GBP 1.93/month before CAC and fixed overhead. Annual GBP 49 is GBP 4.08/month recognized gross for comparison, not GBP 49 of new MRR. Annual upfront cash and earned revenue should not be confused.

| Monthly churn assumption | Simple lifetime | Illustrative contribution LTV |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3% | 33.3 months | GBP 64.47 |
| 6% | 16.7 months | GBP 32.24 |
| 10% | 10.0 months | GBP 19.34 |

Constant-churn approximation: expected months = 1/churn; contribution LTV = monthly contribution/churn. It ignores discounting, growth and changing cohorts. At assumed GBP 10 CAC, payback is about 5.2 paid months. These are sensitivity cases, not industry benchmarks or a DRUM forecast.

At GBP 1,000 monthly fixed overhead, roughly 518 active monthly subscribers are required just to cover it before acquisition spending under this model. A GBP 79 sale contributes about GBP 55.82; after an illustrative GBP 15 CAC, about 25 such sales/month cover the same overhead. This explains why a small developer should not assume recurring revenue is automatically easier.

Rent-to-own spreads cash collection and repeats per-transaction fees, while support/CAC can arrive early. Forecast both default/cancellation and conversion to ownership. A customer who cancels before completion is not equivalent to a perpetual bundle sale.

## Go-to-market, validation and decision gates

1. Release readiness: keep the full regression suite green, validate real Live scenarios and predictable settings transfer, document scope, and finish a dependable signed installer/trial/activation path before broad paid promotion.
2. Audience: start with electronic/beat producers who already own samples and understand sends; do not target deep acoustic realism as the lead claim.
3. Demonstration: show one sound in three spaces, transfer its settings to another sample, then mix the group and inspect the finished mix with Meter.
4. Trial cohorts: measure qualified visits, trial starts, first successful sound, first send edit, settings reuse, paid conversion, support cases and refunds. Ask permission for analytics; do not assume a telemetry system already exists.
5. Price experiment: test GBP 59 vs GBP 79 against comparable traffic and trial experience. Compare net contribution per qualified visitor, not just conversion percentage.
6. Monthly pilot: first test finite bundle ownership payments. Only test a recurring club once there is a funded content schedule and evidence that users return for those additions.

Suggested decision gates are management assumptions: a small cohort of 20-30 observed users for usability; at least 100 qualified completed trials before taking conversion estimates seriously; then several cohorts rather than one campaign before committing to recurring-content staffing. Small samples remain uncertain; no statistical significance is being claimed.

A content-club pilot could aim for 100 paying members and track month-3/month-6 retention, usage and actual servicing costs before scaling. Do not treat 100 members as a profit target: the illustrative unit economics show why it may not fund a substantial monthly workload.

Recommended order: dependable perpetual product, useful Pro bundle, clear owner route, finite monthly ownership option, then optional recurring content. This is a more credible path than building billing complexity first and hoping the subscription itself creates demand.
