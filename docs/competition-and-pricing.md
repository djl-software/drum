## Recommended price

Planning recommendation: **£49 regular / £39 introductory**, sold as a perpetual licence for the initial major version. These are proposed prices, not configured sales offers. Reassess £69 once the full control set, reliable multi-output routing, effects and cross-platform support justify it. Current development previews should remain testing builds.

Drum's strongest proposed benefit is keeping eight named kits, organisational groups and a large key map understandable in one consistent workflow across DAWs. Instrument count alone is a weak sales argument. The commercial release needs responsive sample handling, dependable project recall/relinking, essential per-instrument sound controls and evidence from host testing. Group organisation must save users time compared with their existing workflow.

## Potential annual volume

There is no product-specific traffic, audience, trial conversion, advertising cost or sales history yet. The following figures are **planning assumptions, not industry benchmarks or a statistical forecast**. They refer to the first full commercial year, not the development period.

- Conservative budget: 150–250 licences.
- Working launch target: 300–600 licences.
- Stretch outcome: 1,000–2,000 licences, contingent on distribution, reviews, demonstrations and sustained qualified traffic.

Assume a blended realised selling price of £44, halfway between the proposed £39 launch and £49 regular prices. No discount/refund mix has yet been measured.

| Annual licences | Gross customer sales at £44 |
| ---: | ---: |
| 150 | £6,600 |
| 250 | £11,000 |
| 300 | £13,200 |
| 600 | £26,400 |
| 1,000 | £44,000 |
| 2,000 | £88,000 |

These are gross sales, not profit or take-home income. Tax, payment/store fees, refunds, marketing, support and development costs still need to be accounted for.

A transparent funnel model is: **qualified product-page visits per month × paid-purchase conversion × 12**. At an assumed 1% visit-to-purchase conversion, 300–600 annual sales require about 2,500–5,000 qualified monthly visits. At 1.5%, the 1,000–2,000 stretch range requires approximately 5,600–11,100 visits/month. These conversion percentages are illustrative assumptions, not verified market rates. Real launch ramp-up and seasonal promotions will make monthly results uneven.

Start with the conservative budget. Track product-page visits, trial downloads, activation-to-purchase conversion, refunds and acquisition cost for 60–90 days after launch, then replace the assumptions with measured results. A Windows-only release and a new brand are reasons to keep the budget cautious; a useful trial and clear demonstrations of the kit/group workflow are practical tests of demand.
